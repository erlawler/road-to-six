## Primary LinkedIn launch post

I built **Road to Six**, an evidence-grounded AI product that explores Dallas Cowboys game scenarios without giving betting advice.

The product question was simple: Can a visitor inspect football and market evidence, change assumptions, and understand a traceable probability and its uncertainty in under five minutes?

I owned the product strategy, architecture decisions, data-source evaluation, AI contract, cost controls, measurement plan, and release gates. I used Codex as an implementation and review partner while retaining responsibility for product decisions and approval.

The most important AI decision was what the model should not do:

- Deterministic code calculates the probability.
- Runtime AI explains the drivers, evidence, and uncertainty.
- Schema and semantic checks reject changed numbers or betting guidance.
- A deterministic fallback preserves the experience when AI, quota, or budget is unavailable.
- A $9.50 application cutoff keeps spend under the $10 monthly project limit.
- A reliability receipt makes model, prompt, contract, evaluation, latency, tokens, cost, source, validation, and fallback state inspectable.

The transparent holdout result matters too. The market-aware model improved on football-only Elo, with a Brier score of **0.207 versus 0.220**, but it did not beat the **0.206 market baseline**. The product reports that limitation rather than turning it into a marketing claim.

### 0 to 15 seconds: Frame the product

"Road to Six is an unofficial Dallas Cowboys forecasting lab and a technical product management case study. I built it to make the path from evidence to probability inspectable."

Show the hero, source date, and football-only, market-implied, and market-aware probabilities.

### 15 to 35 seconds: Demonstrate the user job

"A visitor selects a weekly opponent, reviews the most impactful Dallas and opponent players, and changes named scenario assumptions."

Select a game, point to the updated opponent cards, and adjust one Dallas or opponent control.

### 35 to 55 seconds: Show market and model transparency

"The product combines a transparent football baseline with a vig-adjusted market probability. It shows the current source, retrieval time, model version, drivers, and uncertainty."

Refresh odds, run the scenario, and show the model-audit section.

### 55 to 75 seconds: Explain the AI boundary

"Deterministic code calculates the probability. Runtime AI can explain the evidence and uncertainty, but it cannot change the number or recommend a bet. Invalid output, provider failure, or the $9.50 monthly cutoff activates a deterministic fallback."

Show the grounded explanation, uncertainty list, and AI reliability receipt. If Runtime AI is unavailable, identify the fallback and its reason code as the designed reliability state.

### 75 to 90 seconds: Close with product leadership

"The repository includes the product brief, Figma flow, architecture, decision log, measurement plan, data-rights review, and release gates. The model improved on football-only Elo but did not beat the market baseline, and I report that result directly."

End on the GitHub README or artifact index.

## Interview talking points

### Why did you build this?

I wanted a memorable product that demonstrated technical product management beyond a feature mockup. Forecasting created real decisions around data rights, model integrity, AI boundaries, cost, reliability, and responsible use.

### What was the hardest product decision?

The hardest decision was limiting AI. Letting an LLM generate the probability would have looked more advanced but weakened reproducibility and trust. I assigned calculation to deterministic software and explanation to AI.

### What result are you most proud of?

The holdout showed the market-aware model improved on football-only Elo but did not beat the market baseline. Reporting that honestly demonstrates stronger product judgment than claiming an unsupported predictive edge.

### How did you use Codex?

I treated Codex as a delivery and review system. I supplied product boundaries, architecture decisions, acceptance criteria, and publication gates, then used tests and specialist reviews to evaluate the work. I retained product and release accountability.

### What would you do next?

Optional future work could include moderated human research and privacy-preserving funnel analytics. The current portfolio gate uses transparent AI persona validation and makes no claim of human testing.
